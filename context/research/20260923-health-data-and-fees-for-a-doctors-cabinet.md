# Health data and fees for a doctor's cabinet

This is desk research written 2026-09-23 by the session, following the
same convention as the 2026-09-22 cabinet-day research: no cabinet, no
doctor and no comptable has been consulted, Samir's own research
supersedes anything here that conflicts with it. This page does not
cover medical-practice law (the dossier médical, the ordonnance, the
certificat médical) or who inside a cabinet may see a patient's notes;
other agents were assigned those and this page does not repeat them.
Two structural problems cut across every finding below and are stated
once here rather than at each occurrence. First, the JORADP PDF of loi
18-07 and of its 2025 amending law could not be read article by article
in this pass: both files fetched as unreadable binary streams rather
than extractable text, so every article number below comes from a
secondary source (a law firm alert, a compliance blog, a legal-analysis
site) quoting or paraphrasing the article, not from this session reading
the Journal Officiel directly. Second, loi 18-07 of 10 June 2018 was
amended and completed by loi n° 25-11 du 24 juillet 2025 partway through
the period these sources were written in, so a number of blog posts
describe the 2018 text alone, some describe the 2025 text alone, and a
few describe both without always saying which article belongs to which
law. Article numbers are marked with the law they were found attached
to; where a source gave no law, that is stated.

## What this changes for what gets built, or whether a cloud relay may run

**Health data is a donnée sensible, which is the heavier ANPDP track,
sourced to loi 18-07 art. 18.** Article 18 states a principle of
interdiction for processing sensitive data, defined as data revealing
racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical
convictions, trade-union membership, or data relating to health
including genetic data, with an exception only for reasons of public
interest indispensable to the exercise of rights the article names
(legal-doctrine.com, "Le traitement des données sensibles en droit
algérien", quoting art. 18 directly). A cabinet's patient file is health
data in the plain sense of that definition. Whether the ANPDP places a
given treatment under déclaration (roughly ten days to process) or
autorisation préalable (roughly two months, extendable to four) is the
authority's own call made on reviewing the déclaration dossier, and
sensitive data, interconnections and transfers are named as the classes
that get pushed to autorisation (Intervalle Technologies, "Loi 18-07:
protection des données en Algérie"). **Sourced.**

**A secondary source claims some health-related processing sits outside
the law's scope entirely, but no article number for this was found.**
Intervalle Technologies' overview of loi 18-07's scope states plainly
that "quelques traitements échappent au texte: les activités purement
personnelles ou domestiques non communiquées à des tiers, ceux relevant
de la défense et de la sécurité nationales, les bases de données
judiciaires, ainsi que certains traitements de santé, suivi thérapeutique
individuel, contrôle assurantiel, études menées par le personnel
médical." If accurate, an individual doctor's own therapeutic follow-up
of a patient, arguably the core of what the clinic module's patient file
exists to record, could be outside loi 18-07's declaration and
authorisation regime for that specific purpose, while a cabinet's other
processing (appointment booking, billing, a patient-facing app) would
not be. This session could not locate the article carrying this
exclusion in either the 2018 or the 2025 text, could not corroborate it
against a second source, and could not confirm whether it survived the
2025 amendment, which explicitly widened the law's scope in other
directions (judicial and security processing). Given the size of the
consequence, this is flagged as the single most consequential unresolved
fact in this page rather than treated as settled. **Sourced claim, article
not found, corroboration not found: treat as unconfirmed.**

**A délégué à la protection des données (DPO) is now a named statutory
role, but whether a one-doctor cabinet must appoint one is unclear.**
Article 4 of loi 25-11 inserts an article 41 bis into loi 18-07 reading,
per legal-doctrine.com's direct quote: "Tout organisme public ou privé
mettant en œuvre un traitement de données à caractère personnel est tenu
de désigner un délégué à la protection des données personnelles." Read
alone this is universal. A décret exécutif 25-350 is reported (by
itsolutions.dz and a WebSearch synthesis, neither independently
corroborated here) to narrow the duty to public bodies, businesses
processing sensitive data at large scale, and organisations whose data
processing is their main activity. A solo or small cabinet processing
its own patients' records is sensitive-data processing by definition,
but whether it is "large scale" in the décret's sense was not found
either way. **Inferred that the question turns on décret 25-350's exact
wording, which this pass did not read; the duty itself is sourced, its
scope for a small cabinet is not.**

**Breach notification is five working days to the ANPDP, sourced to art.
43 as it now reads after the 2025 amendment.** Legal-doctrine.com states
directly: "L'article 43 de la nouvelle loi impose désormais au
responsable du traitement une obligation stricte de notification à
l'ANPDP en cas de violation des données personnelles, dans un délai
maximal de 5 jours ouvrables," extending to notifying the affected
person when the breach can affect their privacy, unless the ANPDP finds
adequate mitigations already in place. Loi 25-11 also adds an article 41
bis 3 requiring a "carnet automatisé des opérations", an automated log
of who created, read, modified or deleted a record in the file, framed
explicitly as evidence for ANPDP audits. **Sourced**, and directly
relevant: the audit_log table the split plan already treats as
trade-independent kernel infrastructure is not just good practice here,
it is close to what art. 41 bis 3 asks for, though this session did not
verify the two are the same thing in the statute's own terms.

**A cloud relay hosted outside Algeria is not banned outright, but needs
a prior, discretionary ANPDP authorisation that can take up to four
months, sourced to art. 45 bis 13 (loi 25-11) and the déclaration/
autorisation regime generally.** Intervalle Technologies states that
transfers abroad require prior authorisation, are refused when they
would compromise public security or the state's vital interests, and
otherwise require an adequate level of protection at the destination,
evidenced by consent, contractual guarantees, or an international
agreement, until the ANPDP publishes its own list of adequate countries.
Article 45 bis 13, per legal-doctrine.com, has the ANPDP evaluate the
destination's security measures and whether a competent supervisory
authority exists there. None of this describes a hard data-localisation
rule written into the statute itself; several compliance blogs (almawarid,
appel-aura-ecologie, and one WebSearch synthesis attributed to legal
doctrine but not independently corroborated by this session reading that
page directly) characterise the practical effect as "personal data of
Algerian citizens must be stored, processed and protected locally," which
reads as a commentator's summary of the authorisation gate's practical
weight rather than a quoted localisation article. For a médecin's health
data specifically, the sensitive-data class pushes any hosting decision
into the slower authorisation track rather than the faster déclaration
one. **The direct answer to "may Dinar run a cloud relay hosted abroad":
sourced as conditionally yes, subject to a prior ANPDP authorisation this
session estimates (from the same source) at roughly two to four months,
refusable on public-security or state-interest grounds, and requiring
either consent, contractual safeguards, or an adequacy finding for the
host country; not sourced as an outright prohibition, and not sourced as
a hard requirement that the server sit on Algerian soil, though several
secondary sources describe it that way.**

**As the software vendor, Dinar is plausibly a sous-traitant if it hosts
anything, with confidentiality, security, transparency and accountability
duties running from the cabinet as responsable de traitement, but no
article number for the sous-traitant's specific statutory duties was
found.** Intervalle Technologies frames the three roles (responsable de
traitement, DPO, sous-traitant) and states the sous-traitant "agit pour
le compte du responsable et sur ses instructions; il est tenu à des
obligations propres de confidentialité, de sécurité, de transparence et
de responsabilité," and itsolutions.dz's comparison of the two laws adds
that loi 25-11 requires "clauses contractuelles renforcées (sécurité,
confidentialité, réversibilité, notification d'incident, localisation)"
for sous-traitants and SaaS providers, ending what it calls "le 'c'est
chez l'éditeur'" excuse. If Dinar never hosts anything (today's offline
SQLite reality), this duty does not attach; the moment a relay server
holds patient data on Dinar's own infrastructure, or on infrastructure
Dinar operates on the cabinet's behalf, this becomes live. **Sourced in
principle, no specific article number located for what the sous-traitant
contract must contain beyond this framing.**

**A médecin's fee note is now sourced as subject to the droit de timbre
when settled in cash, resolving what the 2026-09-22 research left open.**
Fatoura.app states plainly that "le droit de timbre s'applique aux
factures et documents en tenant lieu utilisés par les professions
libérales ainsi que par les entrepreneurs de bâtiments et de travaux."
Code du timbre art. 100, as reformed by the 2025 loi de finances, taxes
"chaque transaction effectuée avec une facture ou un ticket, dont le
paiement est réalisé en espèces," at a per-100-DA-tranche rate: 1 DA per
tranche up to 30 000 DA, 1.5 DA per tranche from 30 000 to 100 000 DA, 2
DA per tranche above that, with a 5 DA floor and, since 2025, no ceiling
(the previous 10 000 DA cap was removed). A separate article 47 of the
2025 loi de finances (not to be confused with the loi 18-07/25-11 art.
47 discussed above, a coincidence of numbering across two unrelated
laws) exempts payments made by bank transfer, CCP, or electronic payment
terminal. **Sourced**, and it directly overturns the 2026-09-22 research's
open question: a médecin's reçu or note d'honoraires, being a "document
en tenant lieu" of a facture for a profession libérale, is now sourced
as inside art. 100's scope when paid in cash, not outside it by virtue
of being a non-commercial profession.

**TVA on a médecin's acte is best read as the reduced 9% rate under CTCA
art. 23, though this remains contradicted elsewhere and is not settled.**
Two independent searches in this pass (merbouhi.com, and a second,
independent web summary) converged on the same reading: "selon l'article
23 du code des taxes sur le chiffre d'affaires, les actes médicaux
(médecins et vétérinaires) sont soumis au taux réduit (9%)." Other
sources describe "services médicaux et paramédicaux" as exonéré
generally, which may describe a broader institutional exemption
(hospitals, clinics) distinct from the narrower "actes médicaux des
médecins" line item in art. 23, or may simply conflict with it; this
session did not read the CTCA's own text (the douane.gov.dz PDF was not
opened) to settle which. **Sourced toward 9%, contradicted elsewhere,
still a comptable question exactly as the 2026-09-22 research found it,
now with slightly stronger convergence toward the reduced rate rather
than exemption.** Separately, art. 57 of the 2022 loi de finances,
modifying CIDTA art. 217, excludes professions libérales from the taxe
sur l'activité professionnelle (TAP), corroborating the 2026-09-22
research's finding.

**A daily livre des recettes et dépenses is a sourced, specific
bookkeeping duty for a médecin on the régime réel, with a named fine
for not keeping it.** Article 12 of the 2022 loi de finances introduces
CIDTA articles 31 bis and 31 ter, requiring taxpayers under the régime
simplifié des professions non commerciales to hold a livre journal of
professional receipts and expenses, kept daily, stamped and initialled
by the tax service, recording every receipt and every expense; a médecin's
taxable income is the difference between recettes encaissées and
dépenses justifiées. The absence of this register (and a companion
register this pass could not further identify) draws a 50 000 DA fine.
**Sourced**, though this session could not confirm whether "régime
simplifié des professions non commerciales" under CIDTA art. 31 bis/ter
is the same regime a médecin on the (separately sourced) régime réel
falls under, or a distinct intermediate BNC regime; this is a real gap
for docs/features.md to close with a comptable rather than a guess. If
it applies, it is a concrete, buildable feature: a clinic module's
receipts ledger, exportable daily, dated and totalled, is close to what
the statute describes.

**CNAS tiers payant for a conventioned médecin runs through CNAS's own
software and hardware, and no public integration path for third-party
software was found.** A médecin who signs the convention type with CNAS
(the "médecin traitant" scheme, and the equivalent for conventioned
spécialistes) receives, free of charge, a "clé professionnel de santé"
and CNAS's own ChifaMed software to read the Chifa card and process the
claim (cnas.dz, "Conventionnement du médecin traitant"; "Présentation de
la carte CHIFA"). The Chifa card itself has replaced the old paper
"feuille de maladie" for pharmacies and stores the insured's identity,
care-tracking data, and reimbursement history; a newer version stores
the patient's last forty electronic ordonnances. No source found in this
pass describes an API, file format, or integration path by which a
third-party cabinet application (Dinar's clinic module) could submit a
claim into CNAS's system directly; the sourced picture is that a
conventioned cabinet runs ChifaMed alongside whatever else it uses, not
that other software can stand in for it. **Sourced for what exists
(ChifaMed, the Chifa card, the convention type); not found for whether
Dinar could ever integrate with it directly.** Conventionnement
eligibility conditions for a médecin (who may sign, what is required)
were not found in this pass.

## Evidence by topic

### 1. Loi 18-07 and its 2025 amendment (loi 25-11): the ANPDP regime

Loi n° 18-07 du 10 juin 2018 relative à la protection des personnes
physiques dans le traitement des données à caractère personnel was
published in JORADP n° 34 of 10 June 2018 and entered into force, per
the Conseil National des Assurances, in August 2023
(cna.dz, "Entrée en vigueur de la Loi sur la protection des données à
caractère personnel en Algérie"). It created the Autorité Nationale de
Protection des Données à caractère Personnel (ANPDP), an independent
authority (anpdp.dz).

It was amended and completed by loi n° 25-11 du 24 juillet 2025 (28
Moharram 1447), which legal-doctrine.com describes as adding, among
other things: an article 27 bis giving the ANPDP regional audit and
control poles; an article 41 bis making a DPO mandatory; an article 41
bis 2 requiring a registre des activités de traitement (contents:
description of files processed, purposes, categories of data,
categories of recipients, technical and organisational security
measures, kept available to but not published by the ANPDP); an article
41 bis 3 requiring the automated operations log described above;
articles 45 bis 6 through 45 bis 11 setting up a data protection impact
assessment (étude d'impact) and breach-management regime, with art. 45
bis 11 requiring prior ANPDP consultation when the impact assessment
finds a high residual risk; article 45 bis 12 giving the ANPDP explicit
inspection powers, including mandating a member to carry out
verifications, while preserving judicial independence; and article 45
bis 13 setting the cross-border transfer adequacy test described above.
The same source states the amended law also extends the framework to
judicial and security-related processing, previously largely
unregulated, permitting it without prior consent given its nature but
subjecting it to strict controls, particularly around automated
decision-making.

Article 7 of loi 18-07 (unaffected, as far as this pass found, by the
2025 amendment) states the principle that processing personal data
requires the express consent of the data subject, with a minor needing
a legal guardian's authorisation and the data subject free to withdraw
consent at any time; data may only be communicated to a third party for
purposes directly linked to the responsable de traitement's and the
recipient's functions, subject to the data subject's prior consent.
Exceptions to the consent requirement are listed: a legal obligation on
the data subject or the responsable, safeguarding the data subject's
life, performing a contract to which the data subject is party or
precontractual steps taken at their request, safeguarding the data
subject's vital interests when they cannot consent, a public-interest
mission or the exercise of public authority vested in the responsable
or the recipient, or a legitimate interest pursued by the responsable or
the recipient (legal-doctrine.com, "Données personnelles: les
obligations du responsable de traitement en Algérie", quoting art. 7
directly). Article 9 is cited by Intervalle Technologies as the
minimisation principle (data must be adequate, relevant, proportionate
and not excessive, accurate, and kept for a reasonable duration).

Article 18, defining sensitive data and the interdiction principle with
its public-interest exception, is covered above. Article 25 is cited
(by a WebSearch synthesis this session could not independently
corroborate against the primary text) as the article under which the
ANPDP "issues authorisations and receives declarations relating to data
processing treatments, while controlling cross-border transfers to
ensure their compliance." Flagged as sourced-but-uncorroborated.

The déclaration/autorisation/consultation distinction, the roughly
ten-day review for a déclaration and the roughly two-month review
(extendable to four) for an autorisation, and the requirement that a
déclaration describe the responsable's identity and representative, the
nature and purposes of the processing, the categories of people and
data concerned, recipients, retention duration, the service where
rights are exercised, security measures, and any interconnections or
transfers to third parties, all come from Intervalle Technologies'
overview and were not independently re-verified against the ANPDP's own
forms in this pass (the anpdp.dz "Elaboration et mise en ligne des
formulaires" page was fetched but returned only site navigation, not
the forms' content; the forms live behind an authenticated portal at
espace-rt.anpdp.dz per a link inside the Gide law firm PDF).

Penalties are the least consistent part of this research. Several
sources, describing what appears to be the original 2018 penal chapter,
cite fines from 20 000 to 1 000 000 DA and imprisonment from two months
to five years for infractions of the personal-data regime generally,
with a stiffer figure elsewhere (200 000 to 2 000 000 DA, up to five
years) attributed to intentional disclosure of sensitive data, and a
separate figure (60 000 to 200 000 DA, six months to two years) attached
to an unspecified "article 61." Separately, and read directly from
itsolutions.dz's account of loi 25-11, an administrative fine of 500 000
DA under article 47 is described for failures such as ignoring a data
subject's access, rectification or opposition request, or failing to
make a required notification (declaration, modification, local
representative), escalating on recidivism to criminal penalties under
article 64. These figures come from different sources describing
different laws or different articles and were not reconciled against
the primary text in this pass; **treat every specific fine or prison
term above as sourced-but-unreconciled, not as a single settled
schedule.**

### 2. Hosting health data: no equivalent to France's HDS was found

No source in this pass named an Algerian accreditation regime for
health-data hosts equivalent to France's Hébergeur de Données de Santé
(HDS) certification. What exists instead is the general déclaration/
autorisation/transfer-abroad regime of loi 18-07 as amended by loi
25-11, described above, under which sensitive data (health data
included) is pushed toward the slower autorisation track and any
transfer abroad needs the separate cross-border authorisation under
art. 45 bis 13. **Not found**: a rule requiring health data specifically,
as opposed to personal data generally, to sit on Algerian soil or with
an approved host. The "data sovereignty" framing several compliance
blogs use to describe loi 25-11's overall direction is a characterisation
of the transfer-authorisation regime's practical weight, not a quoted
localisation article, and should not be read as a separate statutory
requirement beyond what is described in section 1 above.

### 3. Fees and tax for a médecin libéral

Covered in the lead section above: droit de timbre (Code du timbre art.
100, reformed by the 2025 loi de finances, with a separate 2025 loi de
finances art. 47 exemption for non-cash payment), TVA (CTCA art. 23,
9% reduced rate, contradicted elsewhere), TAP exclusion (2022 loi de
finances art. 57 modifying CIDTA art. 217), and the livre des recettes
et dépenses (2022 loi de finances art. 12 introducing CIDTA art. 31 bis
and 31 ter, 50 000 DA fine for its absence). Not found in this pass:
any Algeria-specific rule listing mandatory mentions on a médecin's
reçu or note d'honoraires (the sources found for this, keltoola.fr and
similar, describe French practice only); any Algeria-specific mandatory
e-invoicing or certified-cash-system rule naming médecins by name (the
2027 caisse-certification obligation under a reported CTCA art. 51 bis
is stated to apply to taxpayers "assujettis à la TVA qui enregistrent
des opérations B2C," which would only reach a médecin if the TVA
question above resolves to "assujetti," i.e. the 9% reading rather than
exonéré, an if this pass cannot close); and any Algeria-specific
electronic-invoicing penalty schedule for a médecin specifically (a
general 25 000 to 100 000 DA fine range for electronic-invoicing
non-compliance was found but not tied to professions libérales
specifically).

### 4. CNAS/CASNOS: tiers payant, the feuille de soins, Chifa

Covered in the lead section above. Additionally: the CNAS page on
Chifa card presentation was fetched but its full text was not read
past the site's navigation in this pass; the sourced facts above (card
replaces the feuille de maladie for pharmacies, stores care-tracking and
reimbursement data, newest version stores 40 electronic ordonnances)
came from the indexed excerpt and a corroborating unblog.fr technical
sheet on the Chifa card. Conventionnement eligibility conditions,
whether tiers payant creates any receivable the cabinet carries against
CNAS beyond the point of service, and whether a private cabinet's own
software could ever read a Chifa card without CNAS's clé professionnel
de santé and ChifaMed, were all not found in this pass and match gaps
the 2026-09-22 cabinet-day research already flagged as unresolved
(tear #17 and #18 in the 2026-09-22 paper-test research, the tiers
payant receivable with no home in Dinar's schema).

## What stays unsourced

- The article number, in either loi 18-07 or loi 25-11, carrying the
  claimed exclusion of "suivi thérapeutique individuel" and similar
  medical processing from the law's scope. This is the single most
  consequential open question in this page.
- Whether a small, single-doctor cabinet counts as processing sensitive
  data "à grande échelle" under décret exécutif 25-350, and therefore
  whether it must appoint a DPO at all.
- A single, reconciled penalty schedule for loi 18-07/25-11 violations;
  the figures found conflict across secondary sources and were not
  checked against the primary text.
- Whether the "régime simplifié des professions non commerciales" that
  CIDTA art. 31 bis/31 ter names is the same regime a médecin on the
  régime réel actually falls under.
- Whether TVA on actes médicaux is 9% (CTCA art. 23) or exonéré; this
  was already unresolved in the 2026-09-22 research and remains so.
- Any Algeria-specific list of mandatory mentions on a médecin's reçu or
  note d'honoraires.
- Conventionnement eligibility conditions for a médecin wishing to sign
  CNAS's convention type.
- Any integration path, public or otherwise, for third-party software
  to submit a CNAS claim without ChifaMed.
- Whether the JORADP text of loi 18-07 or loi 25-11, read directly
  rather than through secondary sources, would confirm or correct any
  article number cited above; this pass could not extract readable text
  from either PDF and every citation above is one level removed from
  the primary source as a result.
