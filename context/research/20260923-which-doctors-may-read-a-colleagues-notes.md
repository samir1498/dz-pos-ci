# Which doctors may read a colleague's notes: associates, remplacants, managers

Scope: this page picks up where
`context/research/20260923-who-sees-the-medical-notes-in-a-cabinet.md`
(the secretary split) and
`context/research/20260923-algerian-law-for-a-doctors-cabinet.md` (the
wider cabinet-law survey, which already covers remplacement generally
and the Saida DSP association-contract material) leave off. It does not
repeat the secretary question, and it does not repeat the fiscal or
hosting findings in
`context/research/20260923-health-data-and-fees-for-a-doctors-cabinet.md`
except where a primary article this session found changes what that
page flagged as unresolved. The question here is narrower: inside a
cabinet where the clinical notes are otherwise doctor-only, may a
second doctor read them, in three shapes (associate, remplacant,
non-doctor manager).

Method note: the earlier research pages record that most decret
92-276 PDFs return raw, undecoded content and could not be read. This
session confirmed why for one of them: the mesrs.dz copy
(services.mesrs.dz/EthiqueDeontologie/DocumentsCharte/2-FR.pdf) embeds
its text in TrueType fonts with no ToUnicode map, so every extraction
tool returns a consistent but meaningless substitution code per
character (31 distinct codes, no digits or spaces resolving either).
That points to a deliberate or incidental copy-protection artifact, not
a scan without a text layer. Cracking that substitution would have
taken more time than a legal research page justifies, so the attempt
stopped once the cause was confirmed. Two things did come through cleanly this
session, read directly rather than through a secondary summary: the
JORADP text of loi 18-11 (already used by the cabinet-law page, but not
for articles 166-172, which this pass extracted and quotes below), and
the JORADP text of loi 18-07 via AFAPDP's mirror, neither of which the
health-data page managed to open as text. A third source, a course PDF
from the Faculte de Medecine de Laghouat that reproduces decret 92-276
article by article starting at Article 1 and citing "l'article 221 du
present decret," extracted cleanly and is quoted below; it is a
secondary source (a teaching document, not the Journal Officiel) but it
is a direct quotation, not an AI summary, and its text for articles
36-41 matches what a search-engine synthesis had already reported in
this same session, which corroborates it independently.

## 1. Loi n° 18-11 du 2 juillet 2018 relative à la santé (primary, read directly)

Extracted and quoted from
https://www.snapo-dz.net/site/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/loi-sante-2018-11.pdf,
the same JORADP N° 46 text file the cabinet-law page used for other
articles. **Sourced, primary.**

- Art. 24: "Toute personne a droit au respect de sa vie privée ainsi
  qu'au secret des informations médicales la concernant, exception
  faite des cas prévus expressément par la loi. Le secret médical
  couvre l'ensemble des informations parvenues à la connaissance des
  professionnels de santé. Le secret médical, peut être levé par la
  juridiction compétente." Only a "professionnel de santé" is inside
  this article's scope at all; nothing in it reaches a non-medical
  employee.
- Art. 169, the article that decides case (a): "Le professionnel de la
  santé exerce sa profession à titre personnel. Il est tenu au secret
  médical et/ou professionnel. Lorsque les professionnels interviennent
  en équipe pour la prise en charge du patient, les informations
  parvenues à l'un des membres de l'équipe, doivent être partagées par
  l'ensemble des membres dans l'intérêt médical du malade." Two things
  in the same article pull opposite ways: the default is personal,
  solo practice and personal secrecy; the exception is a mandatory
  share, not a permitted one ("doivent être partagées"), but it is
  gated on actually being in a team caring for that patient, not on
  sharing a cabinet.
- Art. 166: general conditions to practice a health profession at all
  (nationality, diploma, civil rights, no disqualifying conviction,
  Ordre inscription). Art. 171 refers back to this article for a
  remplaçant's own qualifications, not for a separate remplacement
  procedure.
- Art. 171, the article that decides case (b): "Le professionnel de
  santé, installé à titre libéral, peut se faire remplacer dans les
  conditions prévues par les dispositions de l'article 166 ci-dessus,
  pour cause, notamment de formation ou de congés. Il peut, en outre,
  pour raison de santé, se faire remplacer pour une période maximale
  d'une année renouvelable une fois." A separate paragraph covers
  "suppléance" for a professional holding a permanent public mandate,
  distinct from ordinary remplacement, and states the same rule for
  it: "le professionnel de la santé suppléant exerce sa profession sous
  son identité légale et demeure responsable de ses activités."

## 2. Loi n° 18-07 du 10 juin 2018 (primary, read directly)

Extracted and quoted from
https://www.afapdp.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/Algerie-loi-18-07-du-25-Ramadhan-1439.pdf,
a JORADP N° 34 mirror the health-data page tried and could not open as
text. **Sourced, primary.**

- Art. 5 answers the health-data page's biggest open question ("the
  article number... was not found"): "Les traitements automatisés de
  données à caractère personnel ayant pour finalité la recherche ou les
  études dans le domaine de la santé ainsi que l'évaluation ou
  l'analyse des pratiques ou des activités de soins ou de prévention
  sont soumis à la présente loi, à l'exception des:, traitements de
  données à caractère personnel ayant pour fin le suivi thérapeutique
  ou médical individuel des patients;, traitements permettant
  d'effectuer des études à partir des données recueillies en
  application du point précédent, lorsque ces études sont réalisées par
  les personnels assurant ce suivi et destinées à leur usage exclusif;
 , traitements effectués à des fins de remboursement ou de contrôle
  par les organismes chargés de l'assurance maladie;, traitements
  effectués au sein des établissements de santé par les médecins
  responsables de l'information médicale." So the carve-out is real and
  has an article number, but it is narrower than the health-data page's
  secondary source implied: it excludes individual therapeutic
  follow-up from the *research/study/evaluation* regime this article
  itself sets up, which is a smaller claim than "excluded from loi
  18-07 entirely." A day-to-day patient file kept for ordinary care is
  a fair reading of "suivi thérapeutique ou médical individuel," which
  argues that Dinar's clinic module, used as intended, does not need to
  clear the declaration/authorisation gate the health-data page found
  so heavy, for that specific use. **Sourced, primary; scope of the
  carve-out beyond research/study processing stays a comptable-grade
  question, not settled here.**
- Art. 18 lists the exceptions that let sensitive data (health data
  included) be processed at all: public-interest necessity for the
  responsable's legal functions, express consent, a "disposition légale
  qui le consacre," ANPDP authorisation, defence of vital interests
  where the person cannot consent, processing inside a members-only
  non-profit, data the person made public themselves, defence of a
  legal right, and, at letter (e): "le traitement de données génétiques
  à l'exclusion de celles effectuées par des médecins ou biologistes et
  qui sont nécessaires pour l'exercice de la médecine préventive, des
  diagnostics médicaux et d'administration de soins ou de traitement."
  None of these nine grounds names a non-doctor manager or a cabinet
  administrator; the closest available ground for a doctor keeping an
  ordinary clinical record is "une disposition légale qui le consacre,"
  which loi 18-11 art. 26 and art. 178 (dossier médical duty, already
  sourced in the cabinet-law page) supply. **Sourced, primary, for the
  list itself; the specific "disposition légale" reading is this
  session's inference, not a quoted cross-reference in the text.**

## 3. Décret exécutif n° 92-276 (code de déontologie médicale), secondary, direct quote

Quoted from "Cours de droit médical," Dr Benmediouni Farouk, Faculté de
Médecine de Laghouat, Université Amar Thelidji, 18 April 2016
(http://univ.ency-education.com/uploads/1/3/1/0/13102001/deonto1an03-07code_deonto.pdf),
which reproduces the decree from Article 1 onward, including a
cross-reference to "l'article 221 du présent décret" that ties it to
the same instrument the sibling pages describe. This is a teaching
document, not the Journal Officiel, so it is marked **secondary,
direct quote**, one step better than the AI search summaries the
sibling pages had to rely on, and its text for articles 36-41 matches
an independent search-engine synthesis obtained earlier in this same
session, which corroborates it.

Secret professionnel (Paragraphe 2, the section named in the task):

- Art. 36: "Le secret professionnel, institué dans l'intérêt du malade
  et de la collectivité, s'impose à tout médecin et chirurgien dentiste
  sauf lorsque la loi en dispose autrement."
- Art. 37: "Le secret professionnel couvre tout ce que le médecin,
  chirurgien dentiste a vu, entendu, compris ou lui a été confié dans
  l'exercice de sa profession."
- Art. 38: "Le médecin, le chirurgien dentiste veillera à faire
  respecter par les auxiliaires, les impératifs du secret
  professionnel." This is the only place in either text that mentions
  non-doctor staff at all, and it only binds the doctor to police their
  secrecy, exactly the French R.4127-72 pattern the secretary-split
  page already found by analogy; it grants auxiliaries no access right
  of their own.
- Art. 39: "Le médecin, le chirurgien dentiste doit veiller à la
  protection contre toute indiscrétion des fiches cliniques et
  documents qu'il détient concernant ses malades."
- Art. 40: publication rule (anonymise before scientific publication).
- Art. 41: "Le secret médical n'est pas aboli par le décès du malade,
  sauf pour faire valoir ses droits."

Collaboration between doctors treating the same patient (Paragraphe 4
or 5, devoirs envers les confrères):

- Art. 73: "Quand plusieurs confrères collaborent pour l'examen ou le
  traitement d'un même malade, chacun des confrères assume ses
  responsabilités personnelles. En revanche, le ou les aides choisis
  par le médecin ou le chirurgien dentiste, travaillent sous leur
  contrôle et sous leur responsabilité." Collaboration does not merge
  responsibility; it stays personal to each doctor even while they
  share the same patient's care, which lines up with loi 18-11 art.
  169's "équipe" sharing being about that specific patient, not a
  general merger of files.

Remplacement (Paragraphe 6, exercice en clientèle privée, the section
the task asked about):

- Art. 74: only a same-discipline generalist, or a same-specialty
  confrère or senior resident, may replace a doctor; "les confrères qui
  se font remplacer doivent en informer, sans délais, les sections
  ordinales dont ils relèvent en indiquant le nom et la qualité du
  remplaçant ainsi que la date et durée du remplacement."
- Art. 75: "Une fois, le remplacement terminé et la continuité des
  soins assurée, le remplaçant doit cesser toute activité liée au
  remplacement." The article's own words are about the remplaçant's
  activity generally, not the patient file specifically, but ending
  "toute activité liée au remplacement" is the natural place to also
  end any access the remplacement gave. **Inferred, not a direct
  statement about file access.**

Cabinet de groupe (same section, the article the task asked about):

- Art. 80: "Dans les cabinets de groupe, l'exercice de la médecine, de
  la chirurgie dentaire doit rester personnelle. Chaque praticien garde
  son indépendance professionnelle. Le libre choix du médecin, du
  chirurgien dentiste par le malade doit être respecté. Tout document,
  ordonnance, certificat, doit porter le nom du praticien dont il
  émane et être signé par lui." This is the article that answers case
  (a) directly: sharing a cabinet de groupe or, by extension, a société
  civile professionnelle, does not merge the doctors' practices or
  their patients. Each associate's clinical relationship, and every
  document it produces, stays attached to the doctor who actually saw
  the patient.
- Art. 81: fee pooling between associates is allowed only if they are
  all generalists, or all specialists in the same specialty; a
  financial/administrative rule, not a clinical-access one, but it
  shows the decree does contemplate degrees of integration inside a
  cabinet de groupe without ever touching who may read whose notes.
- Art. 82: a doctor who replaced a colleague for more than three months
  may not install a competing practice nearby for a year afterward
  without agreement, reinforcing that a remplacement is a bounded,
  temporary substitution, not a standing arrangement.

## 4. French comparison, labelled as such

The sibling secretary-split page already quotes CSP L1110-4 in full.
The article that actually defines "équipe de soins" and its shared
secret is a different one, L1110-12, read this session via a search
summary of MACSF's and Doctrine.fr's pages on it (not fetched and read
directly as primary text, so **secondary** even for French law):
membership in an "équipe de soins" requires either working in the same
establishment or a formal coordination structure, being named as such
by the patient, or working in a body with at least one health
professional under a formal cahier des charges; members of the same
équipe "peuvent partager les informations concernant une même personne
qui sont strictement nécessaires à la coordination ou à la continuité
des soins," and those informations "sont réputées confiées par la
personne à l'ensemble de l'équipe." This is a close cousin of loi
18-11's art. 169, gated the same way on actually treating the same
patient together rather than on sharing a building, but the French
text writes the qualifying tests out in far more detail than the
Algerian one-sentence version does. **French comparison, not Algerian
law; useful only to show Algeria's shorter text is heading the same
direction, not to fill its gaps by importing the French detail.**

## Case by case

**(a) An associate in a cabinet de groupe or an SCP.** No default
access. Décret 92-276 art. 80 (secondary, direct quote) keeps each
associate's practice personal and requires every document to carry the
name of the doctor who actually produced it; art. 73 (secondary, direct
quote) keeps responsibility personal even during collaboration. Loi
18-11 art. 169 (primary) opens a mandatory sharing duty, but only
"lorsque les professionnels interviennent en équipe pour la prise en
charge du patient" for that patient specifically, such as a referral, a
consultation, or a formal joint-care arrangement, not by virtue of the
association contract or the shared premises alone. **Sourced (primary
for the sharing gate, secondary direct-quote for the personal-practice
rule); the conclusion that association alone is not enough is this
session's synthesis of the two, not a single article that says so in
one sentence.**

**(b) A remplaçant.** Full access, for the replacement's duration,
under the remplaçant's own name. Loi 18-11 art. 171 (primary) has the
remplaçant "exerce sa profession sous son identité légale et demeure
responsable de ses activités," meaning he is, for that period, the
patient's treating doctor in every sense that matters, not a visitor
borrowing the titulaire's login. Décret 92-276 art. 74 (secondary,
direct quote) requires the section ordinale to be told the remplaçant's
identity and the exact dates; art. 75 requires him to "cesser toute
activité liée au remplacement" once it ends and continuity of care is
assured, which is the textual anchor for ending file access too, even
though the article itself does not say "access" in so many words.
**Sourced (primary for the identity/responsibility rule); the
access-ends-when-the-mandate-ends conclusion is inferred from art.
75's wording, not stated directly.**

**(c) A non-doctor manager.** No basis found anywhere in the three
texts, and this is the most solidly closed of the three questions. Loi
18-11 art. 24 (primary) runs secret médical to "professionnels de
santé"; a manager or administrateur is not one. Décret 92-276 art. 38
(secondary, direct quote) mentions non-doctor "auxiliaires" only to
bind them to secrecy the doctor must enforce, never to grant them
access. Loi 18-07 art. 18's nine grounds for touching sensitive data
(primary) do not include "runs the business side of the cabinet" as
one of them. **Sourced, primary and secondary together; no inference
needed for the conclusion itself.**

## Recommendation for Dinar's permission model

Clinical notes should follow a "doctor" role held per user and scoped
to that patient's actual care relationship, not to the cabinet's one
owner account: a doctor who has never treated a given patient, even
another associate in the same cabinet de groupe, gets no default read
access to that patient's notes, matching décret 92-276 art. 80's
personal-practice rule and loi 18-11 art. 169's team-only sharing gate;
a remplaçant gets the titulaire's own patients' access for the exact
dates of the replacement, writes under his own identity the way art.
171 requires, and loses that access the day the replacement ends; a
non-doctor manager, administrator or accountant never gets clinical
access under any configuration, seeing only identity, scheduling and
billing data, the same split the secretary-split page already
recommended. The cost of building this rather than the current
single-owner-account model: a per-patient care-team relationship in
the schema (not just a global "is a doctor" flag), a doctor identity
tied to a verifiable Ordre number rather than to the shop's one login,
a time-boxed remplacement grant that expires on its own instead of
staying live by accident, and an access-logged audit trail to prove
who read or wrote a note and when, which usefully doubles as the
"carnet automatisé des opérations" loi 25-11 already asks for per the
fees-and-data page, but which is real, non-trivial work regardless.
None of this is worth building before Dinar's clinic module actually
supports more than one doctor account on an installation; on a solo
cabinet, today's single doctor-owner model already satisfies every
rule found here, and the honest recommendation is to defer this build
until a second funded doctor seat is on the roadmap, not to build the
care-team and remplacement-expiry machinery speculatively now.
