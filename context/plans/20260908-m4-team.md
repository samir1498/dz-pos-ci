# M4: team

Ten tasks, written on 2026-09-11 when M3 landed on main. The milestone text
is `docs/roadmap.md` § M4 and the permission list is `docs/features.md` §5.

The shape of it: a `users` table and real sign-in replace the seeded owner
id every row has carried since the first sale, so nothing has to be
backfilled; one permission table in core answers every question about who
may do what; the API takes the actor from a session and each route names
the permission it needs; the screens read the role once and hide what a
cashier may not see, with a grep test forbidding a role comparison in a
screen the way the theme test forbids a theme branch. The M3 carry-ins
below are applied in one pass rather than task by task.

Demo that closes it: a cashier signs in with a PIN, cannot see cost or
override a credit block; the owner reads the audit log of a price change.

In: owner, manager, cashier; PIN on the till, password elsewhere; the
permission list from `docs/features.md` §5; the user on every document; the
audit log. Every PR here gets the `dz-review` pass before merge.


## Carried in from M3 (review rulings, 2026-09-10)

- Anyone may close a supplier fiche, pay a supplier, correct what the shop
  owes one, and reopen a closed fiche; every one of those routes takes the
  actor from the seeded owner id. M4 gates `POST /suppliers/{id}/close`,
  `/payments` and `/adjustments` behind a manager permission, and with them
  the closing branch of `PUT /suppliers/{id}`, because a body carrying
  `active: false` closes a fiche through the same rule the close route uses.

- The four exports, the product template and the two import routes take the
  actor from the seeded owner id like everything else. M4 gates
  `GET /export/{products,sales,customers,suppliers}` and
  `POST /import/products{,/dry-run}` behind a manager permission, and hides
  the "Exports and import" block on the settings screen from a cashier: an
  export is the shop's whole customer list, its whole supplier list and
  every sale it ever rang up, walking out on a USB stick, and an import
  rewrites prices in bulk with one click. `GET /import/products/template`
  goes with them, because a template is only useful to somebody who may
  send one back.

  The labels stay open to everybody: `GET /products/{id}/label` and
  `POST /labels/sheet` print a name, a price and a barcode a customer can
  already read off the shelf, and a cashier relabelling a shelf is the
  person who needs them. The tick column on the product list and the print
  button in the drawer stay visible to a cashier for the same reason.

- Every mutating purchase route takes the actor from the seeded owner id the
  same way. M4 gates `POST /purchases` and `/purchases/{id}/receipts` behind
  the permission that commits the shop's money, and
  `/purchases/{id}/returns` and `/purchases/{id}/close-short` behind the
  ledger-correction permission, because a return and a write-off both lower
  what the shop owes. It also hides the unit cost and the landed cost columns
  on the order page from a cashier: what the shop pays for its stock is not
  something a till operator has any call to read.

- What the rest of M3 leaves for M4 to gate, beyond the supplier and
  purchase routes above (both permissions named there already cover a
  supplier payment and an order's create, receive, return and close):
  `POST /expenses` takes the actor from the seeded owner id and commits
  money out of the drawer the same way a purchase payment does, so it wants
  the same money-committing permission; `POST /stock/recount` (`routes/
  stock.rs` carries a `TODO(M4)` on its user for this reason) rewrites a
  cached quantity on hand from the ledger and wants the ledger-correction
  permission, the way a supplier adjustment and a purchase return already
  do, though a cashier keeps the settings button that reads the last run
  (`GET /stock/recount`), the way they keep the label routes; `PUT
  /settings/theme` is cosmetic and not money or stock, so it stays open to
  everybody unless a shop asks otherwise. `dzpos-seed` needs no route and
  no permission to gate: it is a separate crate neither the API nor the
  desktop depends on, gated by `DZPOS_DEV=1` and the `.dev/` directory
  rather than by who is signed in, and `crates/api/tests/
  no_seed_entrypoint.rs` is what holds that the API never grows a seed
  route for a role to gate.

## Carried in from M2 (review rulings, 2026-09-09)

- A blocked or warned credit sale leaves no audit row: the refusal unwinds
  the sale's transaction, so a row written inside it rolls back. A cashier
  can probe a customer's limit without trace. M4 writes `sale.credit_blocked`
  and `sale.credit_warned` on a second connection, and the override itself
  becomes a permission ("pass a credit block") instead of a flag anyone may
  send.
- Any user can cancel any document of any age (a printed ticket included)
  and issue an avoir up to the facture's total; the two routes take the
  actor from the seeded owner id. M4 gates cancel and avoir behind a
  manager permission, makes the avoir's `reason` required like the
  cancel's, and the documents screen hides the two actions from a cashier.

## Carried in from M1 (review rulings, 2026-09-09)

- The sale API accepts a client-set unit price (`NewSaleLine.unit_price`,
  T3's override for a negotiated price at the till). M1 keeps it because
  the till has one user; M4 gates it behind a permission ("change a price
  at the till") and writes an audit row with the product's stored price
  beside the one used.
- Line and global discounts have no permission and no audit row.
  `docs/features.md` §5 names "give discount above X %" as a permission
  and "discount override" as an audited action; both land here.
- `SEEDED_OWNER_USER_ID` in `crates/api/src/lib.rs` is the user on every
  document until the request carries an identity.
